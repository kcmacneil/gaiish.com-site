Collaboration is what happens after the first answer. Instead of treating the model as a vending machine, you treat it as a colleague who has read nothing about your situation: you supply the material, it drafts, you react to specifics, and the two of you converge — with the model doing the typing and you doing the judging.

How the loop works

  • Give it the room to work in. Documents, data, code, prior decisions. A model with your context beats a bigger model without it.
  • Draft, then react. Ask for a draft early and correct concretely — “rewrite section 2 as three benefit statements”, not “this is off”.
  • Split the roles. Let the model generate options and do the legwork; keep taste, priorities and final judgement on your side.
  • Keep the thread clean. Prune contradictions as you go; an argumentative history degrades every later answer.

Ways to divide the work

  • Draft and critique. One pass writes, a second pass reviews against your criteria and lists only what fails.
  • Grounded conversation. Retrieval puts your own documents into the exchange so answers cite your sources — see Embeddings & RAG.
  • Mixed media. Screenshots, diagrams and recordings are legitimate input for a multimodal model — see Multimodal Systems.
  • Shared standards. A reusable house-style block pasted into every session keeps output consistent across people.

Example

Vague 
That draft isn't right, do it again.
Better 
Keep: the structure and the opening paragraph.
Fix: section 2 is feature-led; I need customer benefits.
Add: the migration timing from the attached plan.
Rewrite section 2 only, three sentences, and list any fact you had to assume.
